How a railway’s radio became the CBC

The real project tracks current events. This sample shows how the record works.

Each card is one documented event and its sources. Events run oldest first.

1923

  1. June 1, 1923

    CNR announces a radio department to serve passengers and hotels

    ExpandCollapse: CNR announces a radio department to serve passengers and hotels

    Sir Henry Thornton, president of Canadian National Railways, announces the CNR Radio Department. W.D. Robb heads it, and W.H. Swift Jr. is director.

  2. July 23, 1923

    Passengers listen to radio aboard a moving train

    ExpandCollapse: Passengers listen to radio aboard a moving train

    A Brooklyn Daily Eagle travel party leaves Montreal’s Bonaventure Station in an observation-lounge car fitted with a radio receiver, and hears a program from Montreal station CHYC. CNR soon equips lounge and parlour cars on 16 of its named trains with receivers, loudspeakers and headsets.

  3. Dec. 30, 1923

    A Montreal-to-Ottawa program is thought to be Canada’s first scheduled commercial network broadcast

    ExpandCollapse: A Montreal-to-Ottawa program is thought to be Canada’s first scheduled commercial network broadcast

    A program from CHYC in Montreal is carried to Ottawa. Broadcasting history accounts describe it as what is thought to be Canada’s first scheduled commercial network broadcast.

1927

  1. July 1, 1927

    Diamond Jubilee broadcast from Parliament Hill reaches an estimated five million listeners

    ExpandCollapse: Diamond Jubilee broadcast from Parliament Hill reaches an estimated five million listeners

    For Canada’s 60th anniversary of Confederation, CNR Radio carries the Parliament Hill celebration, including the new Peace Tower carillon, to 23 Canadian stations. It also reaches NBC stations in the U.S. and the BBC by shortwave. Telegraph and telephone circuits link the country. An estimated five million people hear it.

1929

  1. Sept. 11, 1929

    Royal Commission recommends a national public broadcaster

    ExpandCollapse: Royal Commission recommends a national public broadcaster

    The Royal Commission on Radio Broadcasting (the Aird Commission), appointed in 1928, reports. It proposes a public body that would regulate broadcasting and operate its own stations and programs.

1930

  1. c. 1930

    Edmonton and Calgary stations carry the CNR network

    ExpandCollapse: Edmonton and Calgary stations carry the CNR network

    CNR reaches Alberta listeners by leasing time on local stations: CNRE Edmonton on CJCA, and CNRC Calgary on CFAC and CFCN. CKUA in Edmonton is a network affiliate from 1930–31.

    Date approximate. This record rests on one source, which gives the period, not a day.

1932

  1. May 26, 1932

    Canadian Radio Broadcasting Commission established

    ExpandCollapse: Canadian Radio Broadcasting Commission established

    The Canadian Radio Broadcasting Act establishes the Canadian Radio Broadcasting Commission (CRBC) as a national public broadcaster and regulator. Hector Charlesworth is its chairman.

1933

  1. 1933

    The CRBC buys CNR’s radio stations and begins national programming

    ExpandCollapse: The CRBC buys CNR’s radio stations and begins national programming

    One of the CRBC’s first actions is buying CNR’s stations in Ottawa, Vancouver and Moncton for $50,000. On 23 February 1933 it offers two hours of national programming a week.
